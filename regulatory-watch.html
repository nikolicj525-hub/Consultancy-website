Regulatory Watch

Four EU files. Tracked closely, updated as they move.

This page tracks the status, key dates and latest developments on the EU regulatory files we follow most closely: the EES/ETIAS border regime, the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR), and the Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA) proposal. Each entry links back to the official source.

Last reviewed: 17 September 2026

EES / ETIAS Border Regime

Partly live

Entry/Exit System — Regulation (EU) 2017/2226  |  ETIAS — Regulation (EU) 2018/1240

What it does

Replaces manual passport stamping for non-EU nationals with a biometric Entry/Exit System (EES) at Schengen external borders, and — once live — will require visa-exempt non-EU travellers to hold a pre-travel ETIAS authorisation (a €20 fee, valid up to three years) before arriving at 30 European countries.

Latest development

EES is now fully operational: it began a phased rollout on 12 October 2025 and reached full implementation on 10 April 2026. ETIAS has not yet launched — the European Commission's current public position is that it will start in the "last quarter of 2026," with the exact go-live date to be confirmed several months in advance. As of the most recent reporting, no firm date has been set.

Timeline

  • 12 Oct 2025 EES phased rollout begins at Schengen external borders.
  • 10 Apr 2026 EES reaches full operational status across all border crossing points.
  • Q4 2026 (expected) ETIAS authorisation requirement expected to go live — exact date not yet confirmed.
Sources: EES — European Commission ETIAS — Travel to Europe portal

EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)

In force — application pending

Regulation (EU) 2023/1115

What it does

Requires companies placing certain commodities (cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soy, wood and derived products) on the EU market to carry out due diligence proving the goods are deforestation-free and legally produced.

Latest development

Originally due to apply from 30 December 2024, the application date has now been postponed twice. A targeted revision agreed by the Council and Parliament in December 2025 pushed application to 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators (and micro/small operators already covered by the EU Timber Regulation), and to 30 June 2027 for other micro and small operators. It also simplified due-diligence and data-submission requirements. On 13 July 2026 the Commission adopted a Delegated Act updating the product scope and an Implementing Act setting out the due-diligence Information System, both to support the December 2026 application date. A further Commission simplification package was presented in May 2026.

Timeline

  • 29 Jun 2023 EUDR enters into force.
  • Dec 2025 Council & Parliament agree targeted revision: application postponed and rules simplified.
  • 13 Jul 2026 Commission adopts Delegated Act (product scope) and Implementing Act (Information System).
  • 30 Dec 2026 Application begins for large/medium operators and traders.
  • 30 Jun 2027 Application begins for remaining micro and small operators.
Sources: EUDR — European Commission Council press release, Dec 2025

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR)

In force — applying

Regulation (EU) 2025/40

What it does

Sets EU-wide rules on packaging design, recyclability, minimum recycled content, labelling, reuse targets and restrictions on unnecessary packaging (including e-commerce parcels), replacing the previous Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive with a directly applicable Regulation.

Latest development

PPWR entered into force on 11 February 2025 and became generally applicable on 12 August 2026 after an 18-month transition. As of that date, core rules such as material and space-efficiency requirements apply — for example, empty space in e-commerce parcels must not exceed 40% unless technically unavoidable. Most headline obligations (recyclability grading, minimum recycled content thresholds, harmonized labelling, reuse targets) phase in progressively between 2027 and 2040.

Timeline

  • 11 Feb 2025 PPWR enters into force.
  • 12 Aug 2026 General application begins, incl. material/space-efficiency rules.
  • 2027 – 2040 Phased rollout of recyclability, recycled-content, labelling and reuse requirements.
Sources: PPWR — European Commission Circular Economy platform overview

Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA)

Proposal — Parliament stage

Proposal for a Regulation establishing measures for industrial capacity and decarbonisation in strategic sectors — COM(2026) 100 final

What it does

Aims to build a European framework to accelerate industrial capacity, competitiveness and decarbonisation in strategic sectors (energy-intensive industries, net-zero technologies, automotive), with the ambition of raising industrial manufacturing's share of EU GDP to 20% by 2035, from 14.3% in 2024. Proposed measures include "Made in EU" and low-carbon content requirements in public procurement, foreign direct investment safeguards, streamlined permitting, and Member State obligations to designate Industrial Manufacturing Acceleration Areas.

Latest development

The Commission adopted the proposal on 4 March 2026. It is now going through the ordinary legislative procedure and has been allocated to a joint committee procedure in the European Parliament across the Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE, lead), the Committee on the Internal Market and Consumer Protection (IMCO), and the Committee on International Trade (INTA). On 8 September 2026 the three co-rapporteurs — Anna Cavazzini (Greens/EFA), Christophe Grudler (Renew Europe) and Pierre Jouvet (S&D) — presented their draft report, which proposes raising the "Made in Europe" / low-carbon content threshold in strategic public procurement to 50% by 2036. The file has not yet been adopted; Council negotiations are proceeding in parallel.

Timeline

  • 4 Mar 2026 European Commission formally proposes the IAA (COM(2026) 100).
  • 2026 Allocated to joint committee procedure: ITRE (lead), IMCO, INTA.
  • 8 Sep 2026 Co-rapporteurs present draft report proposing a 50%-by-2036 "Made in EU" threshold.
  • Ongoing Committee negotiations and Council position still in progress — no adoption date set.
Sources: Proposal text — EUR-Lex Legislative procedure file — OEIL Legislative Train Schedule
